MLP/1 · Draft 00 · 12 November 2026

Steadwell Labs

Status: Experimental working draft. Not an IETF submission. Not an implemented, audited, or deployed standard. Implementations that interoperate today may break without notice.

1Abstract

Meshless is an application-layer protocol for discovering, authorizing, and invoking resources across independently administered computing nodes. It separates resource ownership, network connectivity, and delegated authority.

A resource is described by a signed descriptor, reached through an endpoint chosen by its owner, and invoked under a short-lived, scoped grant that may be delegated a bounded number of times. Every invocation is bound to a single-use proof and produces a signed receipt.

Meshless operates without an Internet connection on a local network and over a private network remotely. No blockchain, central registry, commercial account, or particular AI agent is required. Nothing in the protocol requires that the provider and the consumer be administered by the same party.

The protocol does not replace IP, VPNs, TLS, the Model Context Protocol, or operating-system isolation. It is a resource access layer that runs above whatever connectivity already exists.

2Status and terminology

This document is a proposed experimental baseline. It is not an implemented or audited standard.

The key words MUST, MUST NOT, REQUIRED, SHALL, SHALL NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT, MAY, and OPTIONAL in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 [RFC 2119] [RFC 8174] when, and only when, they appear in all capitals.

MLP/1 denotes the proposed major wire version. Draft 00 is not a stable release. Unknown major versions MUST be rejected (§18).

Terms used throughout:

TermMeaning
ResourceA capability hosted by a provider and identified by a resource ID.
DescriptorA signed statement describing a resource: who owns it, who serves it, where it is, and which actions exist.
GrantA signed, scoped, expiring authorization for one subject to invoke named actions on one resource.
ProofA single-use, request-bound signature demonstrating possession of a subject key.
ReceiptA provider-signed record of one invocation and its outcome.
ChainAn ordered list of grants from a root grant to a leaf grant, inclusive.

3Goals and non-goals

3.1Goals

  1. Owner-controlled resources. The owner decides who may do what, for how long, and retains the ability to withdraw that authority.
  2. Least privilege. Authority is expressed as an explicit set of actions on one resource with explicit hard limits, never as ambient access.
  3. Portable identity. Principals are cryptographic keys, not accounts held by an intermediary.
  4. Explicit delegation. Authority may be narrowed and passed on, but never widened.
  5. Transport independence. The same identifiers, grants, and semantics apply over direct HTTPS on a LAN, over a private network, or over a future transport profile.
  6. Local-first operation. A resource on the local network is usable with no Internet connection and no external service.
  7. Verifiable invocation records. Consumers receive provider-signed receipts that bind a request to an outcome.
  8. Interoperability. Existing applications, including MCP servers, can be exposed as resources without modification.

3.2Non-goals

Meshless explicitly does not and does not claim to:

  • replace IP, TLS, VPNs, overlay networks, or routing;
  • replace the Model Context Protocol, identity providers, or authorization servers that already own a domain of authority;
  • provide consensus, settlement, payment, or token issuance;
  • provide operating-system or hardware isolation between resources;
  • guarantee availability, latency, correctness of remote computation, or deletion of data already disclosed to a provider;
  • provide anonymity to any party;
  • hide inputs from the provider that executes them;
  • authorize remote physical actuation (§16).

4Roles and trust

A principal is a human, organization, device, or agent represented by a cryptographic key pair.

  • A provider hosts a resource and enforces policy on every invocation.
  • A consumer invokes a resource on behalf of a principal.
  • An owner controls the root authority over a resource ID.
  • A directory distributes signed descriptors. It cannot grant authority.
  • A relay forwards traffic. It cannot authorize resource access.
  • A transport adapter establishes connectivity. It cannot authorize resource access.

Roles MAY coexist on one node. A provider is commonly also a consumer of other resources.

Trust rules:

  1. Providers MUST authenticate an owner during enrollment using an independently trusted channel (§6.2). Enrollment establishes which key is the root authority for a resource ID.
  2. Possession of a key proves control of that key. It does not prove a legal identity, membership in an organization, or intent.
  3. Unknown keys MUST NOT be trusted solely because they self-sign. A key presented inside a signed object is only as trustworthy as the signer of that object and the enrollment decision behind it.
  4. Network access and resource access are independently enforced. Reaching an endpoint grants nothing.
  5. A directory, relay, or adapter that is compromised MUST NOT be able to cause a grant to be widened or replayed. Compromise of these components is assumed in §16, not excluded.

5Mandatory interoperability profile

5.1MLP/1-HTTP

An implementation MUST support the following profile before claiming baseline interoperability:

ElementRequirement
TransportHTTP over TLS 1.3 [RFC 8446]
EncodingUTF-8 JSON [RFC 8259]
SignatureCompact JWS [RFC 7515] with EdDSA over Ed25519 [RFC 8037]
CanonicalizationRFC 8785 JSON Canonicalization Scheme [RFC 8785]
Identifier hashSHA-256
Base64urlRFC 4648 §5, unpadded
TimeInteger Unix seconds

TLS certificates MUST be validated against an explicitly configured trust anchor: a public CA root, a private CA, or a pinned certificate enrolled for that provider. There MUST be no insecure fallback, no "proceed anyway" path, and no plaintext mode. A private VPN or overlay network alone is insufficient; it does not remove the TLS requirement.

TLS termination MUST occur at a trusted provider boundary. An untrusted relay MUST NOT terminate application TLS. A relay that terminates TLS is, for the purposes of this protocol, the provider.

5.2Non-mandatory profiles

QUIC transport, libp2p transports, CBOR encoding, alternative signature algorithms, and payload encryption are future profiles. They are not required for baseline interoperability and MUST NOT be assumed present. Section 18 governs their addition.

6Identity, keys, and enrollment

6.1Key identifiers

A key identifier is the string:

mlp:key:<thumbprint>

where <thumbprint> is the base64url encoding, without padding, of the SHA-256 digest of the RFC 8785 canonical form of the public Ed25519 JWK containing exactly the members crv, kty, and x [RFC 7638]. For Ed25519 the JWK is {"crv":"Ed25519","kty":"OKP","x":"<base64url public key>"}.

Example: mlp:key:z6Mk... is not produced by this construction; identifiers are 43 base64url characters after the prefix, e.g. mlp:key:7Xk9....

Key identifiers are stable and are used as iss, sub, aud, and kid values. A key identifier MUST be recomputed from the public key and MUST be compared as a byte string.

6.2Public key distribution

JWS objects carry a key identifier, not a key. To make delegation chains verifiable without a directory, MLP/1 defines these carriers:

  1. A descriptor MUST include owner_jwk and provider_jwk, the public Ed25519 JWKs of the owner and the provider. iss MUST equal the key identifier of owner_jwk; provider MUST equal that of provider_jwk.
  2. A grant MUST include sub_jwk, the subject's public Ed25519 JWK, and sub MUST equal that JWK's key identifier.
  3. A verifier MUST recompute the thumbprint of any embedded JWK and MUST reject the object if it does not match the referenced key identifier.

owner_jwk is required because a key identifier is a hash and cannot reconstruct the key it names. Without it, a descriptor received as a file or a QR code could not be checked by a party that had not already enrolled the owner, and the descriptor exchange paths in §8 would be unimplementable. The resource ID still binds the key: an attacker who substitutes owner_jwk changes the thumbprint, which no longer matches the owner component of rid.

Consequently, a leaf subject's key is learned from the leaf grant, and a provider's signing key is learned from the descriptor that authorizes it. No online key lookup is required. Embedding keys also bounds the trust placed in any key distribution channel: a directory that substitutes a key changes the thumbprint and invalidates the object.

An enrollment record binds a resource ID to an owner root key and a provider key, recorded by the provider through a trusted channel. Enrollment records and revocation records MUST be persisted across restarts.

6.3JWS headers

Every MLP/1 JWS object is in compact serialization, and its protected header MUST contain:

  • alg: the string EdDSA;
  • kid: the signer's key identifier, equal to the payload's iss;
  • typ: the media type for the object (mlp-resource+jwt, mlp-grant+jwt, mlp-proof+jwt, or mlp-receipt+jwt).

The protected header MUST contain exactly these three members and no others. A verifier MUST reject a header that carries any additional member, including jwk, jku, x5u, x5c, crit, cty, or b64, whose alg is anything other than EdDSA, whose typ is not the expected value, or whose kid does not equal iss. Verifiers MUST NOT fetch key material from a URL named in a signed object.

A closed header is cheap and removes a class of attack: there is no key-selection member for a verifier to consult, no payload-processing flag to invert, and no extension point that could change what the signature covers.

6.4Enrollment procedure

Enrollment is out of band with respect to this protocol. A conforming implementation MUST provide some procedure by which:

  1. the owner presents its root public key to the provider over an independently trusted channel (local presence, an operator command, a signed enrollment bundle delivered in person, or an organizational identity system);
  2. the provider records the binding of resource ID to owner key;
  3. the owner learns and records the provider's key identifier from the enrollment, so that the first descriptor it accepts is not merely self-asserted.

Enrollment is a human decision. Implementations SHOULD require an explicit confirmation showing the full key identifier and resource ID, SHOULD render thumbprints in groups of four characters for comparison, and SHOULD support revocation of an enrollment.

6.5Key management

  • Agent keys SHOULD be distinct from human keys. An agent's key SHOULD live in OS-backed secure storage (Keychain, DPAPI, TPM, secure enclave) and SHOULD be marked non-exportable where the platform allows.
  • Key rotation requires re-enrollment in Draft 00. There is no in-band rotation message. A rotated key is a new key identifier and therefore a new subject.
  • On suspected compromise the owner MUST revoke outstanding grants (§11, §14), MUST revoke the enrollment if the root key is affected, and MUST re-enroll with a new key. Revocation of a key does not retroactively invalidate receipts or already-disclosed data.
  • Private keys MUST NOT appear in descriptors, grants, proofs, receipts, logs, process arguments, or agent prompts.

7Resource identifiers and descriptors

7.1Resource identifiers

A resource ID is:

mlp:res:<owner-thumbprint>:<uuid>

where <owner-thumbprint> is the owner's key identifier without the mlp:key: prefix, and <uuid> is a lowercase RFC 9562 UUIDv4 in 8-4-4-4-12 form [RFC 9562].

Resource IDs are stable across endpoint changes, provider restarts, and transport changes. A resource ID identifies an *authorization domain*: the owner and the resource. It does NOT identify a host, address, or process, and MUST NOT be used as a network locator.

A consumer MUST verify that the <owner-thumbprint> component equals the thumbprint of the descriptor iss, and MUST reject the descriptor otherwise.

7.2Descriptor claims

A descriptor is a compact JWS with typ mlp-resource+jwt, signed by the resource owner. Its payload MUST contain:

ClaimTypeSemantics
vintegerProtocol version. MUST be 1.
ridstringResource ID (§7.1).
issstringOwner key identifier. MUST be the signer.
owner_jwkobjectOwner public Ed25519 JWK (§6.2).
providerstringProvider key identifier.
provider_jwkobjectProvider public Ed25519 JWK (§6.2).
seqintegerNonnegative safe integer, monotonically increasing per rid.
iatintegerIssuance time.
expintegerExpiry time. MUST be ≤ iat + 86400.
kindstringOne of inference, mcp, storage, sensor, compute, application.
actionsarrayNonempty array of unique strings. Action names allowed on this resource.
endpointstringHTTPS origin, e.g. https://host:8443. No path, query, fragment, or credentials.
metaobjectOPTIONAL. Free-form, non-normative. MUST NOT contain secrets.

Additional rules:

  1. The owner MUST sign the descriptor. A provider signature is not a substitute; iss is the owner.
  2. A consumer MUST reject a descriptor that is expired, not yet valid (iat in the future beyond 30 seconds of skew), whose rid owner component does not match iss, whose iss does not match the thumbprint of owner_jwk, whose provider does not match the thumbprint of provider_jwk, whose endpoint is not an https origin, or whose actions is empty, duplicated, or contains pattern syntax.
  3. A consumer MUST maintain a high-water mark of seq per resource ID and MUST reject a descriptor whose seq is less than the stored mark. Equal seq with different bytes MUST be rejected.
  4. Descriptor expiry MUST be no more than 24 hours after issuance. A shorter lifetime is RECOMMENDED for resources reachable from untrusted networks.
  5. kind selects an action schema profile (§12). kind is advisory for routing; enforcement is by explicit action name. Unknown kind values MUST be rejected by baseline implementations.
  6. meta MUST be treated as untrusted display data. An implementation MUST limit its size and MUST NOT act on it programmatically.

7.3Discovery and authorization are separate

Possession of a valid descriptor conveys no authority. A descriptor states that an owner authorizes *the existence and location* of a resource. Invocation requires a grant (§9). An implementation MUST NOT treat successful discovery as permission to invoke anything.

8Discovery

Baseline discovery is the explicit exchange of signed descriptors over a channel the participants already trust: a QR code, a file, a clipboard, a local administrator, an out-of-band message, or a directory.

8.1Well-known endpoint

A provider MAY expose:

GET /.well-known/meshless

This path is provisional and unregistered. The response is {"v":1,"descriptors":["<compact JWS>", ...]} with Content-Type: application/json and Cache-Control: no-store.

The endpoint MUST require a valid proof (§10) from a subject the provider has been configured to serve, or MUST otherwise restrict access to a configured allowlist. Public listing is opt-in, off by default, and MUST be a separate operator decision from serving. A provider that does not wish to be discoverable MUST return 404, not an authentication error, so as not to confirm existence.

8.2Directories

A directory MAY distribute descriptors. It is untrusted infrastructure:

  1. It MUST preserve descriptor bytes. A directory that re-signs or re-encodes a descriptor destroys its value.
  2. It SHOULD minimize metadata: no query logs keyed to identities, no per-subject association, no retention beyond what serving requires.
  3. Consumers MUST NOT automatically connect to arbitrary discovered URLs. Endpoint origins MUST be approved by local policy (enrollment, allowlist, or explicit user action) before the first connection.
  4. A directory MUST NOT be able to make a consumer reach an endpoint that local policy has not approved.

8.3Local and optional discovery

LAN discovery (mDNS/DNS-SD), rendezvous services, and libp2p-based discovery are optional extensions. When used:

  • discovered descriptors MUST still be verified in full;
  • DNS resolution and HTTP redirects MUST NOT be used to bypass egress restrictions;
  • an implementation MUST NOT downgrade to HTTP or to an unverified TXT-record key when direct verification fails.

9Capability grants

9.1Form

A grant is a compact JWS with typ mlp-grant+jwt. Its payload MUST contain:

ClaimTypeSemantics
vintegerMUST be 1.
jtistringRandom UUIDv4, unique per grant.
issstringIssuer key identifier.
substringSubject key identifier.
sub_jwkobjectSubject public Ed25519 JWK (§6.2).
audstringProvider key identifier.
ridstringResource ID.
actionsarrayNonempty array of unique action names. No wildcards.
iatintegerIssuance time.
nbfintegerNot-before time. MUST be ≥ iat and < exp.
expintegerExpiry time. MUST be ≤ iat + 86400.
delegatebooleanWhether this grant may be delegated.
depthintegerRemaining delegation depth, 0–3.
limitsobject{calls, bytes, max_ms}, each a positive safe integer.
parentstring or nullnull for a root grant; otherwise the base64url SHA-256 of the exact parent compact JWS ASCII bytes.

A grant MUST NOT contain wildcard actions, regular expressions, or action patterns. Every action is a literal string that MUST appear in the descriptor's actions array.

delegate MUST equal depth > 0. A grant with remaining depth is delegable; a grant with no remaining depth is not. The two claims cannot disagree, so a verifier never has to reconcile "may delegate" against "cannot delegate further".

9.2Root grants

A root grant ( parent = null ) MUST be signed by the resource owner whose key identifier is the owner component of rid, and MUST satisfy:

  • aud equals the descriptor provider;
  • actions is a subset of the descriptor actions;
  • depth ≤ 3;
  • delegate is true if and only if depth > 0;
  • the grant is issued while a valid descriptor for rid exists.

A root grant issued to a subject other than the owner's own key is the ordinary case: the owner is granting a consumer.

9.3Delegation

A child grant has a non-null parent. Its issuer MUST be the parent's sub, and it MUST satisfy every one of:

  1. aud equals the parent aud (audience is invariant under delegation).
  2. rid equals the parent rid.
  3. actions is a subset of the parent actions.
  4. [nbf, exp) is contained in the parent's [nbf, exp).
  5. Each of limits.calls, limits.bytes, limits.max_ms is less than or equal to the corresponding parent limit.
  6. The parent has delegate = true.
  7. depth equals parent depth − 1.
  8. delegate equals depth > 0, so a child with depth 0 has delegate false.
  9. parent equals the base64url SHA-256 of the exact parent compact JWS ASCII bytes.

Rules 3, 4, and 5 are conjunctive at every hop, so a chain can only narrow. A verifier MUST evaluate all nine rules for every link. A verifier MUST NOT accept a chain in which any link is missing: all ancestors MUST be presented, from root to leaf.

9.4Validation requirements

A provider MUST reject a grant chain unless:

  1. every signature verifies against the key identified by that object's kid, and every embedded JWK's thumbprint matches its key identifier;
  2. the root grant is signed by the enrolled owner key for rid;
  3. every delegation rule in §9.3 holds;
  4. the leaf aud equals this provider's key identifier;
  5. the leaf rid equals the resource being invoked;
  6. the leaf actions is a subset of the descriptor actions for rid;
  7. the current time is within every link's [nbf, exp);
  8. no link's jti is revoked, directly or by ancestor revocation;
  9. the chain depth does not exceed 4 grants (root plus at most three delegations).

An implementation MUST reject an object containing an unknown claim that is marked critical, and MUST reject an unsupported constraint. Unknown non-critical claims that carry no semantics MAY be ignored, but an implementation SHOULD record them.

9.5Quota semantics

limits are hard aggregate ceilings, not per-request hints:

  • calls counts admitted invocations, including invocations that later fail, time out, or are rejected for schema errors after admission;
  • bytes counts application-body bytes: the UTF-8 length of the canonical form of the invocation's input object plus that of its result object. Headers, the proof, and transport framing are excluded. Measuring the canonical form rather than the raw request bytes makes the charge identical across implementations regardless of whitespace or member order;
  • max_ms bounds a single invocation's wall-clock duration.

Quota is enforced per link including all ancestors. A provider MUST, for each invocation, atomically reserve against the leaf and every ancestor, and MUST reject the invocation if any link lacks remaining capacity. Counters MUST be persisted across restart. Concurrent requests MUST NOT be able to overshoot a ceiling; read-modify-write without atomicity is non-conforming.

An owner policy MAY impose stricter limits than a grant states. The effective limit is the minimum of every applicable ceiling.

9.6A grant is not a bearer credential

Possessing the bytes of a grant confers no authority. Each invocation requires a proof signed by the current leaf subject's private key (§10) and a session (§11). Grants are therefore safe to transport over channels that are confidential but not necessarily trustworthy, and interception of a grant does not permit its use.

10Wire encoding and request authentication

10.1JSON

Implementations MUST parse JSON strictly:

  • duplicate member names MUST be rejected;
  • invalid UTF-8 MUST be rejected;
  • NaN, Infinity, and -Infinity MUST be rejected;
  • integers outside the IEEE-754 safe integer range MUST be rejected;
  • a top-level value that is not an object MUST be rejected where an object is required;
  • trailing content after the value MUST be rejected.

10.2Canonicalization

Signed JSON payloads MUST be serialized with RFC 8785 before signing. Object members are sorted by UTF-16 code unit, numbers use the ECMAScript Number::toString form, strings use JSON escaping, and no insignificant whitespace is emitted.

A verifier MUST re-canonicalize the decoded payload and MUST reject the object if the result is not byte-identical to the received payload bytes. This prevents signature-valid but byte-different encodings from producing different hashes for the same semantic object, which would otherwise break the parent chain and the grant_hash binding.

Signatures are verified over the original JWS signing input (base64url(header) || "." || base64url(payload)), never over a re-serialized object.

10.3Encoding details

  • base64url without padding, without line breaks, canonical alphabet.
  • JWS payloads in compact serialization only; no detached payloads, no multiple signatures, no unprotected header.
  • Time values are integer Unix seconds.
  • Providers MUST have a reliable clock. A provider whose clock is uncertain beyond a few seconds MUST reject traffic rather than serve it.
  • Expired objects MUST be rejected with no grace period. A skew allowance applies only to proof iat (§10.4).

10.4Proof of possession

Every authenticated request carries a proof JWS with typ mlp-proof+jwt, signed by the leaf subject. Its payload MUST contain:

ClaimTypeSemantics
vintegerMUST be 1.
issstringLeaf subject key identifier. MUST be the signer.
audstringProvider key identifier.
jtistringRandom UUIDv4.
iatintegerSigning time.
noncestringNonce issued by this provider for this subject and resource.
methodstringUppercase HTTP method.
uristringExact requested origin plus path, with no query and no fragment.
body_hashstringbase64url SHA-256 of the exact request body bytes, or of the empty string when there is no body.
grant_hashstringbase64url SHA-256 of the exact leaf grant compact JWS ASCII bytes, or the empty string for owner-only operations (§11.6).

The provider MUST compare iss against the session or chain subject, aud against its own key identifier, method and uri against the request exactly as received, body_hash against the exact bytes received, and grant_hash against the exact leaf grant. The provider MUST accept a proof only if |now − iat| ≤ 30 seconds. The provider MUST consume the nonce atomically and single-use, and MUST reject a reuse with 409 replay.

This is a Meshless proof format inspired by DPoP [RFC 9449]. It is not DPoP and does not claim RFC 9449 compliance.

11HTTP endpoints and session lifecycle

11.1Common requirements

  • Requests use POST with Content-Type: application/json, except the well-known discovery endpoint (§8.1) which uses GET.
  • No redirects. A provider MUST NOT answer 3xx on any MLP path, and a client MUST NOT follow one.
  • Maximum request body is 1 MiB unless a resource profile sets a lower limit.
  • Responses are JSON with Cache-Control: no-store.
  • Requests carry Meshless-Proof: <compact JWS> except where noted.
  • Every POST body is proof-bound via body_hash, so an intermediary cannot alter it, but it is still plaintext to the provider.
  • A request without a valid proof MUST be rejected before grant evaluation, so that grant structure is not disclosed to unauthenticated callers.

11.2POST /mlp/v1/challenge

Body: {"rid": "<resource id>", "subject": "<key id>"}

Returns {"nonce": "<32 random bytes, base64url>", "expires_at": <unix seconds>}.

The nonce is valid for 60 seconds from issuance, MUST be bound to that resource and subject, and MUST be single-use. Issuance MUST be rate-limited per source address and per subject. The endpoint is unauthenticated by design and MUST NOT reveal whether the resource or subject exists.

11.3POST /mlp/v1/sessions

Body: {"chain": ["<root grant>", "<child>", ..., "<leaf>"]} Header: Meshless-Proof.

The provider validates the chain (§9.4), the descriptor, the proof binding, and policy, then returns {"sid": "<32 random bytes, base64url>", "expires_at": <unix seconds>}.

The sid is bound to the leaf key identifier and to the exact chain. Session expiry is the earlier of the leaf grant exp and 15 minutes after creation. Before issuing an sid, the provider MUST verify grant_hash equals the hash of the leaf grant in that same request body.

11.4POST /mlp/v1/invoke

Body: {"sid":"<sid>","request_id":"<uuidv4>","rid":"<rid>","action":"<action>","input":<object>} Header: Meshless-Proof, obtained with a fresh challenge.

On every invocation the provider MUST re-validate: session validity, chain validity and expiry, revocation, action membership, quotas, input schema, response size, and the proof bindings. Session establishment is not a durable authorization decision.

Success returns {"request_id":"<uuidv4>","result":<object>,"receipt":"<compact JWS>"}.

11.5POST /mlp/v1/sessions/close

Body: {"sid":"<sid>"}. A fresh proof is required.

Invalidates the session and cancels cancellable work. Returns {"closed": true}.

11.6POST /mlp/v1/revoke

Body: {"rid":"<rid>","jti":"<grant jti>"} Header: Meshless-Proof signed by the enrolled owner, with grant_hash set to the empty string.

The provider MUST persist the revocation before returning {"revoked": true}. Revocation of an ancestor invalidates all descendants. Revocation is by jti and therefore applies to exactly one grant in the chain.

An owner MAY additionally revoke every grant for a resource by disabling the resource locally, and MAY revoke an enrollment (§6.4). Owner control MUST remain available independently of any consumer session and without network access to any third party.

11.7Binding rules for proofs

Operationnonce subjectgrant_hash
sessionsleaf subjectleaf grant hash
invokeleaf subjectleaf grant hash
closeleaf subjectleaf grant hash
revokeownerempty string
discoveryrequesting subjectempty string

The provider MUST reject a reused nonce, a nonce bound to a different subject or resource, a proof for a different method or URI, and a body whose hash does not match. A session identifier alone confers no authority; an sid presented without a valid proof is an unauthenticated request.

12Invocation and receipts

12.1Processing

For each invocation the provider MUST, in order:

  1. authenticate the proof and consume the nonce;
  2. resolve the session and re-validate the chain and revocation state;
  3. confirm the action is in the leaf grant;
  4. validate the input against the action schema for the resource kind;
  5. reserve quota atomically across the chain;
  6. invoke the handler under a timeout and a response byte limit;
  7. record the outcome, emit a receipt, and return.

The handler MUST run with least privilege relative to the provider: it MUST NOT be able to reach resources the provider has not explicitly granted to it. Implementations SHOULD sandbox handlers and MUST NOT grant a handler ambient network or filesystem access by default.

12.2Baseline action schemas

kind: inference — action infer Input {"prompt": string, "max_tokens": positive integer}. Result {"text": string}. A provider MAY impose lower maximums than the request and MUST reject max_tokens above its own ceiling with 400 invalid_request.

kind: mcp — actions tools.list and tools.call

  • tools.list input {}, result {"tools": [{"name": string, "description"?: string}]}
  • tools.call input {"name": string, "arguments": object}, result {"content": <object>}

A provider MUST enforce a separate, explicit tool allowlist and MUST validate arguments against the tool's schema. Upstream MCP lifecycle and version negotiation are the provider's responsibility. A grant for tools.call MUST NOT expose any tool outside the allowlist, and an allowlist MUST NOT be derivable from a grant.

kind: storage, sensor, compute, application require separately specified, versioned action schemas. Draft 00 does not define them. An implementation that exposes these kinds MUST publish its schema and MUST NOT invent schema semantics that collide with a future revision.

No provider MUST forward arbitrary URLs, shell commands, filesystem paths, or headers supplied by a consumer. Streaming and asynchronous jobs are deferred to a future profile.

12.3Idempotency

A provider MUST deduplicate request_id per resource per leaf subject until the leaf grant expires, rejecting a repeat with 409 duplicate_request instead of executing twice. Rejecting a duplicate is preferred to returning the previous result, because a cached result may be indistinguishable from a fresh one to the caller.

A crash after dispatch may leave the outcome unknown. MLP/1 makes no exactly-once claim and MUST NOT be described as providing one.

12.4Receipts

A receipt is a compact JWS with typ mlp-receipt+jwt, signed by the provider, containing:

ClaimSemantics
vMUST be 1.
ridResource ID.
issProvider key identifier.
subLeaf subject key identifier.
jtiLeaf grant jti.
request_idThe invocation's request_id.
actionAction invoked.
iatCompletion time.
status"ok" or "error".
request_hashbase64url SHA-256 of the RFC 8785 canonical form of the input object.
result_hashbase64url SHA-256 of the canonical form of the result object, or null on failure.
usage{"calls":1,"bytes":n,"elapsed_ms":n}.

Receipts attest that a provider reports having executed an invocation. They do not attest intent, correctness, or that the reported work happened.

Receipts MUST NOT contain prompts, tool arguments, results, credentials, or internal addresses. A client MUST verify the receipt signature against the provider key it learned from the descriptor, and MUST verify rid, sub, jti, and request_id bindings before treating a receipt as authentic.

Error messages MUST NOT expose credentials, internal addresses, stack traces, or the existence of a resource to a party not authorized to learn it. Returning 404 unknown_resource where a caller lacks authority is RECOMMENDED over 403, except where the caller is authenticated for that resource.

Clients SHOULD retry transient failures with bounded exponential backoff and jitter. A client MUST NOT automatically retry an invocation whose execution outcome is unknown under a new request_id; retry requires the same request_id and an explicit decision by the caller.

14Revocation and offline behavior

The provider is the authoritative enforcement point. It MUST check locally persisted revocation and expiry before each invocation and SHOULD cancel active work when a grant is revoked.

A remote owner cannot guarantee immediate revocation while disconnected from the provider. Local policy MAY require online freshness checks or shorter leases for sensitive resources. Implementations MUST NOT describe revocation as globally immediate; the honest guarantee is "no new invocation is admitted after the provider learns of the revocation".

Local operation on a LAN or on the same device requires no Internet, but still requires valid credentials, a correct clock, and trusted TLS. Offline operation MUST NOT bypass policy, MUST NOT silently route private data to a cloud service, and MUST NOT fall back to a weaker transport.

After a disconnect, reconnection requires fresh session establishment. Queued side-effecting operations require renewed approval by the person or policy that authorized them. Data already disclosed to a provider cannot be recalled by revocation.

15Transport adapter contract

This section is an implementation API contract, not a wire protocol.

An adapter implements:

connect(endpoint, policy) -> connection
status() -> {state, endpoint, detail}
disconnect() -> void
provision(plan) -> result        // optional

policy includes allowed destinations, an egress allowlist, and an explicit user-approval requirement for network changes. Adapters MUST:

  1. preserve TLS validation end to end (§5.1);
  2. report failure rather than degrade;
  3. avoid silent public fallback from a private or local path;
  4. keep provisioning credentials outside grants, proofs, and agent prompts.

Direct HTTPS on a LAN or an existing private network is the baseline adapter and requires no extra component. A provider's access control remains mandatory regardless of which adapter carried the request; a "trusted network" is not an authorization mechanism.

A doxx.net adapter may provision tunnels, DNS, and firewall policy through that service's documented APIs with dedicated credentials. Compatibility with doxx.net MUST NOT be claimed before it has been tested and reported (§17).

16Security and privacy considerations

16.1Threats in scope

Stolen keys, malicious consumers, malicious providers, compromised directories, compromised relays, replay, SSRF through descriptors or redirects, quota races, prompt injection against agent consumers, tool misuse, metadata leakage, and denial of service.

16.2Mitigations

Key-bound proofs; short leases; explicit enrollment; signed descriptors with owner-bound resource IDs; TLS 1.3 with an explicit trust anchor; single-use nonces; strict body and URI binding; strict JSON parsing and canonicalization; per-resource isolation; egress allowlists on handlers; atomic quota; rate limits; minimal logs.

Implementations MUST reject algorithm confusion (none, HMAC-with-public-key, key-type substitution), keys fetched from signed URLs, and unknown critical headers.

16.3Non-claims

Providers see plaintext inputs and outputs. MLP/1 does not provide confidential computing, zero-knowledge execution, or end-to-end encryption of resource payloads, and MUST NOT be described as providing them without a separate verified profile. Relays observe metadata even when they cannot read content. Revocation cannot recall disclosed data.

16.4Physical actuation

There is no remote physical actuation in the baseline. Robotics telemetry is read-only. A future actuation profile requires local safety interlocks, an independent emergency stop that does not depend on the protocol path, and hardware-level limits. Draft 00 MUST NOT be used to move, energize, or unlock physical systems.

16.5Data handling

Receipts and logs SHOULD be locally controlled with configurable retention. Implementations SHOULD minimize what is logged: key identifiers, action names, outcomes, and byte counts, not payloads. Identities and resource inventories MUST NOT be published by default.

18Extensions and governance

Major version changes MAY break compatibility. A receiver MUST reject an unknown major version. Optional extensions require explicit negotiation and MUST NOT weaken any baseline check; a negotiation that would relax a check is non-conforming.

Proposed future profiles, none required: WebAuthn enrollment, HeartBadge or Bitcoin-anchored proofs, Trellis-backed receipts, libp2p discovery, streaming and asynchronous invocation, remote attestation, post-quantum signatures, metering and payments, and confidential execution.

The project maintains public proposals, a changelog, a threat model, test vectors, and implementation reports. Code is licensed Apache-2.0 and this specification is licensed CC BY 4.0. No IANA registrations are requested in this draft; all names, paths, and media types are provisional.

AProposed doxx.net pilot (non-normative)

See docs/doxx-pilot.md for the expanded engineering plan. Summary: connect two enrolled devices over a private doxx.net network; host one narrowly scoped inference or MCP resource on one; exchange the signed descriptor; issue a five-minute grant; invoke from the second device; then demonstrate a denied action, revocation, and LAN operation with the external network removed. Use the same resource ID and grant semantics on both transports. Measure setup time, invocation overhead, revocation enforcement, and failure behavior against a direct LAN baseline.

Do not use customer data or robot actuation. Network credentials remain in local secret storage. Success requires recorded positive and negative results, not a UI demonstration.

Proposed collaboration: Steadwell Labs supplies the protocol and a resource gateway; doxx.net provides technical review and a supported connectivity integration, subject to mutual agreement. No compatibility claim is made until tested.

CChanges in Draft 00

Ratified from the pre-draft working notes; recorded here rather than in CHANGELOG.md because no earlier revision was published.

  1. Key distribution is now in-band and self-verifying. Descriptors carry owner_jwk and provider_jwk; grants carry sub_jwk. Without this, a verifier could resolve a kid only by trusting a lookup it had no way to validate, and a grant chain could not be verified offline. See §6.2.
  2. Canonical payloads are mandatory on the wire, not just before signing. Verifiers reject a payload that is not byte-identical to its canonical form. Otherwise two encodings of the same grant produce two parent hashes and grant_hash bindings for semantically identical authority.
  3. Grant actions are explicitly subsetted against the descriptor. The draft constrained grants against their parent but not against the descriptor, so an owner could issue a grant for an action that was never declared.
  4. The audience is bound to the descriptor as well as the grant. §9.2 and §9.4 now require aud to equal the descriptor provider, closing a grant-reuse path between providers that share a resource ID.
  5. Chain length is explicitly bounded to four grants (root plus three delegations), matching the 0–3 depth range.
  6. Discovery requires a proof and returns 404 rather than an auth error, so that concealment is the default.
  7. Duplicate request_id is rejected rather than answered from cache.
  8. Baseline schemas for non-inference/MCP kinds are explicitly unspecified rather than implied, so implementations cannot silently diverge.
