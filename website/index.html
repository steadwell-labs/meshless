THE OPEN RESOURCE ACCESS PROTOCOL

Relay access,
not ownership.

The internet connected computers. Meshless connects their capabilities.

An open protocol for discovering, authorizing, and accessing distributed computing resources across independently owned infrastructure.

01 / Why

Addressability is not access.

Every meaningful resource on a network — a GPU, a private dataset, a sensor, a tool server — belongs to someone. Reaching it today means a shared account, a network position, or a copy of the data. Each grants more than it should, and none of them states precisely what was allowed.

The separation

Who owns a resource, who can reach it, and who may use it are three different questions.

Meshless separates them. A relay forwards traffic but cannot authorize access. A directory distributes signed descriptors but cannot grant authority. A grant is a signed, scoped, expiring statement about specific actions — not a bearer credential, and not a property of the network.

Nothing in the design requires a blockchain, a central registry, a commercial account, or a particular AI agent. Two devices on the same LAN are a complete deployment.

03 / How it works

Four signed objects, one live proof.

Every step is verifiable by the party that receives it. Nothing depends on a shared secret, an ambient network position, or a provider's promise about who the caller is.

  1. Enroll a key

    The provider authenticates the owner over an independently trusted channel and records a binding between the resource ID and a root Ed25519 key. From then on, possession of that key — not membership in a network — is what owner control means.

    §6 — Identity and enrollment

  2. Publish a signed descriptor

    The owner signs the resource descriptor: kind, allowed actions, HTTPS endpoint, sequence number, and an expiry of no more than 24 hours. Descriptors can travel by QR code, file, trusted directory, or a well-known endpoint — but discovery never implies authorization.

    §7 — Identifiers and descriptors · §8 — Discovery

  3. Issue a scoped grant

    The owner signs a grant bound to one consumer key, one resource, one audience, and an explicit action list, with call, byte, and time limits. Grants expire within 24 hours and can be attenuated to a child grant — never widened.

    §9 — Capability grants

  4. Invoke and receive a signed receipt

    The consumer proves possession of the leaf key for each request with a single-use nonce bound to the method, URI, body hash, and grant hash. The provider verifies the whole chain, enforces quotas atomically, runs the handler, and signs a receipt attesting what it executed.

    §10 — Request authentication · §12 — Invocation and receipts

The draft does not claim exactly-once execution. A crash after dispatch may leave an outcome unknown, and a receipt attests to provider-reported execution — not to correctness.

04 / The model

The relay is not the authority.

A provider hosts a resource and enforces policy. A consumer invokes it. The network in between can forward signed objects, but it cannot turn reachability into permission.

06 / Ecosystem

One layer of a family of open efforts.

Meshless concerns access. Adjacent initiatives concern identity, memory, and intelligence. They are independently specified: MLP/1 requires none of them, and lists them only as optional future profiles where the draft mentions them at all.
